Early learners

  • Read a short story aloud, retell it, and draw a favorite moment.
  • Practice letters on handwriting paper. Count ordinary objects, sort by shape, and make repeating patterns.
  • Try memory pairs, simple nature searches and drawing prompts.
  • Explore NASA’s illustrated Sun story with an adult.

Elementary school

  • Read a chapter and write who, what, where and why.
  • Use square grids for place value, multiplication arrays, fractions and area.
  • Choose a NASA or Junior Ranger booklet; draw or explain three things learned.
  • Keep an observation journal: weather, shadows, plants or the Moon.

Middle & high school

  • Use the local encyclopedia to investigate a question; record the article title, evidence and uncertainties.
  • Plot measurements, compare linear and logarithmic scales, or create a map to scale.
  • Compare two texts, write an argument, and revise it after a discussion.
  • Study a repair guide or technical explanation before planning a practical project with appropriate supervision.

A simple learning day

  1. Pick one question or goal together.
  2. Read, listen or explore for a comfortable length of time.
  3. Make something: notes, a drawing, an explanation, a calculation or a small project.
  4. Discuss what worked and what to explore next.
  5. Allow time for movement, rest and play.

Adjust the level and format to the learner. Paper, read-aloud, hands-on activities and conversation all count. Keep learning records on the family’s device, not the public library.
