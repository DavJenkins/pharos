    Date, time and greeting variables

    Use [daypart], [weekday], [date:MM/DD], [time:h:mm A]. Example: Good [daypart]! Have a lovely [today] [nextdaypart], a happy [tomorrow] tomorrow, and a wonderful [nextweekpart] ahead!

    Formats: YYYY YY MMMM MMM MM M DD D dddd ddd HH H hh h mm m ss s A a. For example [date:ddd, MMM D], [time:HH:mm:ss]. Quote literal words with single quotes.

    The viewing device’s local clock and timezone are used. Morning: 5–11:59; afternoon: noon–16:59; evening: 17–20:59; night: 21–4:59. Values update automatically. Any weekday name (such as [Monday]) means the current weekday; any daypart name (such as [morning] or [afternoon]) means the current daypart. [date], [MM/DD] and example dates such as [10/26] use MM/DD. Unknown variables and code stay literal.

    Today: [today] (weekday), [today:MM/DD]. Next: [nextdaypart], [tomorrow] (weekday), [tomorrow:MM/DD]. [weekpart] means week on Monday–Friday and weekend on Saturday–Sunday; [nextweekpart] gives the upcoming opposite. Date ranges: [thisweek], [nextweek] (Monday–Sunday), [thisweekend], [nextweekend] (Saturday–Sunday). Add a format such as [nextweekend:MMM D]. This weekend is the current weekend on Saturday/Sunday, otherwise the coming one; next weekend always starts in the future.
